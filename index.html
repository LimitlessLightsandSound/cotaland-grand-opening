battery first

Almost no cable on the ground

The Pixel Bricks, the AX5s, the AX9s and the QuikSpots are all battery-powered. That means no cable runs through guest paths across most of this property, far less trip hazard and matting, and a much faster build. Our crew charges the full fleet overnight between the 25th and 26th, so night two starts on full batteries.

low profile

Sleek Schedule 40 uprights

Where we do need height, it is Schedule 40 pipe on 20" bases, sandbagged, rather than truss towers or tripod stands. It is a slimmer, cleaner vertical that disappears into a themed environment instead of announcing itself, and it takes up almost no footprint in a walkway.

precision

QuikSpots on the F1 cars

The cars get (16) Astera QuikSpots, zoomed tight for highly focused highlights. These are pin-point fixtures rather than washes, so the cars read as the hero objects in that area with clean falloff and no spill onto the surrounding build.

Everywhere else

Static, warm, and the same everywhere.

Outside the Safari Zone the approach is deliberately consistent: efficient stage lighting on each activation, with highlight accents to give the space depth. Set once, looks right all night, nothing to operate.

design rule 01

No underlight. Ever.

Every fixture on this property is placed above or across a subject, never below it throwing shadows upward. Underlight is what makes a face look like a campfire ghost story, and on a family opening that is the one look we will not accidentally create.

design rule 02

Depth, not drama

Each area gets a key level plus highlight accents that separate foreground from background. That is what stops a space reading flat under a single wash. It is the same technique used to light a stage set, applied to an activation guests walk through.

design rule 03

Fun and approachable

Warm, bright, saturated where it should be. No harsh angles, no horror-movie gels, no dark corners. Parents with a stroller should read every path as obviously safe and obviously inviting, and kids should want to run toward it.

overnight

Batteries charged between nights

The Astera fleet comes off the property or goes to a secured charging point after night one and is back at full charge before doors on the 26th. We handle it; we just need somewhere lockable with power.

cadence

Set once, then hold

Outside the Safari Zone every area is set and locked on the build days. Show nights are about watching it, not running it. That is what keeps the show crew at two people instead of eight.

Beyond the opening

We are not here for one weekend.

Being honest about the motive: we want to be COTALAND's lighting partner, not the company that filled a gap in September 2026. That changes how we would price and behave from here, and it is worth putting in writing.

value engineering

A session whenever you want one

We will take this scope apart with you at any point, right down to the week of the show. Swap a fixture, change a count, test what an area looks like for less. We will show you what each move does to the number before you decide. No charge for the conversation, now or on any future job.

transparency

Changes priced before they happen

Anything that moves the scope gets quoted and approved before we execute it, and the whole budget gets re-baselined each time so you always hold one current number. You will not find a line on an invoice you are seeing for the first time.

the long game

Year one is the expensive one

The first build of anything costs the most, because nobody has done it before. We keep the plot, the counts, the positions and the lessons on file. Next season we clone it forward instead of rebuilding from memory, and that is where a repeat partner starts actually saving you money.

local

Ten minutes up the road

We are Austin-based, with our shop and warehouse a short drive from the circuit. For a property running events year-round that means same-day response on anything that breaks, changes or gets added late, without a travel line attached to it.
